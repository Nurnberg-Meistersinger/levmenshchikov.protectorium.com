Why claims should compile

Most claims workflows still look like a stack of rituals: screenshots, PDF exports, investigator notes, threaded emails, and long arguments about whether the evidence is complete. The process pretends to be rigorous, but it is really a chain of manual trust.

A better model is to treat a claim like something that can compile. The inputs should be structured, the rules should be explicit, and the output should only exist if the evidence satisfies the policy conditions. That does not remove judgment from the system, but it moves judgment to the right layer: policy design, data collection, and verification logic.

In cyber insurance this matters even more because the strongest evidence is often the most sensitive. You cannot ask companies to hand over their infrastructure every time something goes wrong. The workflow has to keep raw data close to the edge and move only the minimum verifiable signal upstream.

Product design changes once you accept that framing. Fewer forms. Fewer explanations. More deterministic evidence handling. More confidence in what a system can prove on its own. That is the direction I find most interesting: less performance of trust, more evidence that arrives already shaped to be checked.
